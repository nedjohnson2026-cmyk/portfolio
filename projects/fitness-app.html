ClutchFit

An AI-assisted fitness and nutrition app that I designed and built end to end. It generates personalized training programs, logs workouts offline, tracks calories and macros, and keeps people motivated with XP, quests, streaks, and challenges with friends. Train smarter, level up.

Overview

Most fitness apps do one thing: log workouts or count calories or give you a plan. I wanted one app that does all three for people 13 and up who want structured programming, nutrition tracking, and a reason to keep showing up. I built the whole stack myself: the mobile app, the API, the database, subscriptions, and the App Store release.

  • Offline-first workouts. An active session writes every set to SQLite on the phone first, then syncs to Supabase when you tap "Save & Finish." A dropped signal at the gym never loses a workout.
  • Security by default. Row Level Security is on for every table, so users can only read their own data. The privileged service key stays on the server, never in the app.
  • Automatic setup. A database trigger on signup creates each user's profile, settings, level, and streak rows, so a new account is ready immediately.
  • 18 SQL migrations built the schema step by step: auth, subscriptions, exercises, programs, sessions, nutrition, recipes, analytics, social, quests, and notifications.

Features

Onboarding & targets

A multi-step onboarding collects goals, body stats, experience, and diet, then calculates calorie and macro targets with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation.

Workout logging

A readiness check, then weight, reps, and RPE for every set, an auto-starting rest timer, custom workouts, and a summary of duration, volume, and sets.

Exercise library

1,301 searchable exercises, custom exercises, 39 substitution pairs, and 8 guided templates (5/3/1, Push Pull Legs, Starting Strength, and more).

Nutrition & recipes

A daily calorie bar, macro bars, water tracking, four meal sections, food search, weekly weigh-ins, and a recipe browser with tag filters.

Progress analytics

19 nightly jobs compute streaks, XP, leaderboards, and body composition. Premium charts show volume and calorie trends over time.

Quests, XP & levels

33 quests from bronze to platinum, 30 milestones (10 of them secret), badges, and three streak counters: workout, calories, and protein.

Social

Friends, head-to-head challenges with leaderboards, and an activity feed with emoji reactions and preset encouragements.

Subscriptions & reminders

A RevenueCat paywall (Premium $7.99/mo or $79.99/yr), six themes, and local reminders for workouts and streaks at risk.

Program generator

The program builder uses Daily Undulating Periodization (DUP), which rotates intensity and rep ranges day to day instead of week to week. I wrote it as custom algorithmic code, not an AI prompt. It generates a full hierarchy: a macrocycle, its microcycles, the program weeks, and every planned workout. The app displays these on macrocycle and week-plan screens.

Right now the generator builds the first three weeks (the accumulation phase) using placeholder exercise picks. Full 12-week phase logic and exercise selection by muscle group are next.

App Store release

Shipping to the App Store taught me more than building the features did. Apple rejected the app several times, and each rejection meant reading crash logs and changing how the app starts up:

  • Broken support and privacy links (Guideline 1.5). I fixed them by publishing real Privacy Policy, Terms, and EULA pages on GitHub Pages.
  • A launch crash on iPad (Guideline 2.1). I wrapped native initialization in error handling, added an error boundary, and sent the reviewers detailed notes plus a demo video.
  • Crashes on iOS/iPadOS 27. Apple's crash logs traced the crash to native modules starting up. I disabled React Native's New Architecture, moved RevenueCat out of app launch, replaced a native slider with a pure-JavaScript one, and upgraded to Expo SDK 57 for a newer JavaScript engine.
  • Build tooling problems. I fixed dependency conflicts, build numbers App Store Connect rejected as already used, a Node version mismatch, and a firewall blocking Expo's local dev server.
Lessons: test on the newest OS before submitting, keep the native plugin config minimal, make native-module startup failures non-fatal, and ask App Review for crash logs early.
